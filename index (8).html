Our story

By

Meena Raghavan, founder
· Published 3 November 2026

We started in a ten-square-metre shed behind a filter coffee stall, with a second-hand drum roaster and a stubborn idea: South Indian beans deserve to be tasted on their own, not buried under chicory. Today we buy directly from 14 estates, and every bag carries the harvest date.

Our house blend is Rs 650 now Rs 520 for 250 g*. Chemistry of a good cup: H2O at 93 °C, about 18 g of coffee per 300 ml. *Price valid until stock lasts. GST included.

Roast notes: jaggery, orange peel, dark chocolate. Judges at the CQI table scored our Coorg lot 86.5 points. As our head roaster says, the bean tells you when it’s ready.

The first sip tasted like my grandmother’s kitchen at 6 a.m.

— A customer in Adyar

In coffee, a cupping is a structured tasting where roasted samples are brewed side by side. The Japanese word for coffee is 珈琲(kōhī). Our Arabic-reading customers see قهوة on the door, and we occasionally print backwards labels for fun. This underlined text marks a misspelling-check: “recieve”.

Brew guide

South Indian filter

  1. Add 3 tbsp medium-fine grounds to the upper chamber.
  2. Pour 200 ml of water just off the boil.
  3. Wait 12 minutes for the decoction to drip.
  4. Mix with hot milk and sugar to taste.

You will need

  • A steel filter set
  • Fresh whole beans

Not sure where to start?
Ask us at the counter, we love questions.
Why does my coffee taste bitter?

Usually the grind is too fine or the water too hot. Try a coarser grind first.

How long do beans stay fresh?

Best within four weeks of the roast date, stored airtight away from light.
